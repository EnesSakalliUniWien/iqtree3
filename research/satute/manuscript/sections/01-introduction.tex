Model-based phylogenetics combines tree search with branch-support measures such as the bootstrap and approximate likelihood-ratio tests \cite{felsenstein1985confidence,hoang2017,anisimova2006,minh2020iqtree}. These measures quantify reproducibility or local preference for a split. They do not test whether repeated substitutions have erased the historical dependence connecting the groups on its two sides. A branch can therefore have high support even when the alignment no longer contains recoverable signal across that branch.

Saturation is the progressive loss of historical information as multiple substitutions accumulate at the same sites \cite{philippe2011,ho2004}. Alignment-wide indices and locus filters can identify heavily saturated data \cite{xia2003index,duchene2021}, but deep phylogenies often contain a mixture of informative and uninformative genes, regions and rate categories. A branch-specific diagnostic is needed to localize that loss.

SatuTe (Saturation Test) generalizes saturation from pairs of sequences to the two subtrees separated by a branch \cite{manuel2025satute}. Its null hypothesis is subtree independence, represented by independently evolving subalignments. Rejection indicates phylogenetic signal and classifies the branch as phylogenetically informative; otherwise, the branch is considered saturated at the chosen threshold. SatuTe thus complements branch support: one asks whether a split is reproducible, the other whether the alignment still connects its two sides.

The test uses the spectral decomposition of a reversible substitution model \cite{levin2017,tavare1986}. For a site pattern \(\partial\), partial likelihood vectors \(L(\partial_A)\) and \(L(\partial_B)\) give subtree probabilities \(P(\partial_A)=\langle L(\partial_A),\pi\rangle\) and \(P(\partial_B)=\langle L(\partial_B),\pi\rangle\). With eigenvalues \(\lambda_0=0>\lambda_1\geq\lambda_2\geq\cdots\) and left eigenvectors \(h_i\), the coherence coefficient is
\begin{equation}
C_{\partial i}
=
\left\langle \frac{L(\partial_A)}{P(\partial_A)},h_i\right\rangle
\left\langle \frac{L(\partial_B)}{P(\partial_B)},h_i\right\rangle .
\label{eq-coherence}
\end{equation}
Published SatuTe retains only the slowest non-stationary component. We ask whether faster components should contribute while they persist on a finite branch, and derive weights from their decay relative to the slowest component. We also correct the infinite-branch null for explicit invariant sites, which retain ancestral dependence even as the focal branch tends to infinity.

We benchmark both changes against the original Jukes--Cantor (JC) design and in paired nucleotide and protein simulations with three decision rules and independent truth-labelled calibration. We also revisit ribosomal-protein two-domain and 16S rRNA three-domain Tree-of-Life examples \cite{hug2016,williams2D,woese1990}. The aim is not to choose a Tree-of-Life topology, but to identify which branches and alignment regions retain detectable historical signal under a fitted model.
