\subsection*{The corrected invariant null restores false-positive control}

Having established exact continuity with the original JC test, we used exact enumeration to validate the finite-branch \(+I\) null. This exploratory \(+I+G4\) and \(+I+R4\) grid is distinct from the fixed-model publication benchmark below. Across 270 model--branch--alignment-length cells, 10.8 million paired alignments were evaluated. The likelihood identity in \cref{eq-soft-mixture-identity} held to \(1.55\times10^{-11}\). The refined score had exact null expectation below \(1.53\times10^{-16}\) in absolute value, whereas a counterfactual score that treated invariant sites as independent retained expectations of 0.0354--0.2176.

At the ordinary one-sided \(N(0,1)\) threshold, counterfactual rejection averaged 0.900; refined rejection averaged 0.0492. Split-sample empirical thresholds brought held-out rejection near 0.05 for both formulas, but did not make the counterfactual statistic a standard-normal pivot: at \(t=10{,}000\), its threshold rose from 3.15 at 100 sites to 18.47 at 10,000 sites, whereas the refined threshold remained between 1.62 and 1.69.

\subsection*{The refined invariant statistic gains power at the detection boundary}

After independent calibration, the refined statistic had the larger exact null-centered separation in 245 of 270 cells and equal separation in the remaining 25. Gains formed a diagonal ridge as longer alignments moved the detection boundary to longer branches (Fig.~\ref{fig-invariant-null}). The maximum \(+I+G4\) gain was 0.116 at \(t=60\) and 10,000 sites (bootstrap 95\% interval, 0.111--0.122); the maximum exploratory \(+I+R4\) gain was 0.163 (0.157--0.170). Supplementary Table S1 reports maxima by alignment length.
