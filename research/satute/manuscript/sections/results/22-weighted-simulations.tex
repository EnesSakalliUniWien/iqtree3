\subsection*{Exploratory power curves and the publication suite}

The legacy simulation curves below are retained as an exploratory overview of the
JC and Tree-of-Life rate-heterogeneous settings. They are not the source of the
primary publication claims: those claims use the corrected 1,000-replicate
publication grid, fixed GTR+F/G4/+I+G4 specifications, explicit model-
misspecification comparisons, and independent truth-labelled calibration
reported in Section~24. Under JC, the published and weighted curves coincide,
providing the expected zero-difference control. The publication-scale GTR
effects and their paired uncertainty intervals are reported from the verified
schema-v2 tables in Section~24.

\begin{figure}[p]
  \centering
  \makebox[\textwidth][c]{\includegraphics[width=1.08\textwidth]{figures/figure_power_curve_summary.pdf}}
  \caption{\textbf{Exploratory full-alignment simulation curves.} Curves show
  the fraction of replicates called phylogenetically informative in the
  true-tree fixed-length scenario. These legacy curves provide context for the
  corrected publication-scale analyses in Section~24; under JC, the published
  SatuTe and eigenvalue-weighted curves overlap.}
\end{figure}

The earlier 1,000-replicate curves give the qualitative pattern that motivates
the corrected suite: larger separations occur for short alignments and long
branches, while decisions converge as alignment length increases. Because the
legacy grid used a different cell definition and model set, its cell-wise
percentages are not pooled with the publication estimates; the complete
publication-scale estimates, intervals, and McNemar tests are given in
Section~24.

\subsection*{Decision changes are localized}

The branch-neighborhood analysis likewise remains an exploratory localization
check. It tests whether changes are concentrated on the focal branch rather
than its neighbours; the corrected branch-audit tables in the publication
suite provide the authoritative branch-level evidence and are used for the
paper's claims.
