\begin{abstract}
Sequence saturation can erase the historical signal for a branch despite high conventional support. SatuTe tests whether the subtrees separated by a branch remain statistically coherent. We extend its spectral statistic to all non-stationary directions of a reversible substitution model, weighted by their persistence, and derive the correct null for invariant-site mixtures. The extension reduces exactly to published SatuTe under Jukes--Cantor. In paired publication-scale simulations, weighting improved detection chiefly for short alignments at the long-branch decision boundary; the largest matched GTR+F+G4 increase was 57.6 percentage points under taxon Bonferroni. Across 1,728 matched-protein cells, differences were positive in 831 and negative in none. Independent truth-labelled experiments verified false-discovery control. Tree-of-Life and HCV examples localized broad rRNA and codon-position-specific losses, respectively. SatuTe therefore complements branch support by identifying where alignments retain recoverable historical signal.
\end{abstract}
