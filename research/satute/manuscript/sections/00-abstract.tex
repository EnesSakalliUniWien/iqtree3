\begin{abstract}
Sequence saturation can leave a branch in a phylogeny without enough signal to justify the split it represents. SatuTe tests this by estimating a coherence coefficient for the second largest eigenvalue of the substitution model. We ask whether, under rate-heterogeneous Tree-of-Life models, the remaining non-stationary directions should contribute to the same test, and how an explicit invariant-site component changes the infinite-branch null. The relative eigenvalue-weighted statistic keeps the SatuTe null hypothesis and reduces to SatuTe under the Jukes--Cantor model. For \(+I\) mixtures, the invariant category remains jointly distributed across an infinite focal branch and must enter only the null denominator. The publication-scale fixed-model suite uses GTR+F, GTR+F+G4 and GTR+F+I+G4, with 1,000 paired replicates per grid cell and independent truth-labelled exact-null and mixed-null calibration. In the matched GTR+F+G4 grid, eigenvalue weighting increased the largest unadjusted informative-call fraction by 24.1 percentage points, by 57.6 points under taxon Bonferroni, and by 41.4 points under Benjamini--Yekutieli. Across 1,728 matched-protein cells the weighted difference was positive in 831, zero in 897 and negative in none. Mixed-null GTR power was 92.9--98.9\%, with empirical FDR at most 0.39\%. The paired effects were concentrated in short alignments and long branches, while JC remained an exact zero-difference control. The older dense \(+I+R4\) calculation is retained as an exploratory methodological analysis; all primary rate-heterogeneity claims use the fixed \(+G4\) and \(+I+G4\) specifications.
\end{abstract}
