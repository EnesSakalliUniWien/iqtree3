Please consider our Article, “Branch-specific detection of phylogenetic signal with SatuTe,” for publication in *Nature Biotechnology*.

Phylogenetic workflows routinely report whether a branch is strongly supported, but not whether the alignment still contains recoverable historical information across that branch. SatuTe addresses this distinct prerequisite by testing statistical coherence between the two subtrees separated by an individual branch. The present study extends SatuTe beyond its slowest spectral component, derives the correct null for invariant-site mixtures, and validates both changes in paired publication-scale nucleotide and protein simulations.

Three findings give the work broad methodological relevance. First, eigenvalue weighting improves detection specifically at the short-alignment, long-branch decision boundary and reduces exactly to published SatuTe under Jukes–Cantor. Second, an invariant-site category remains ancestrally dependent across an infinite focal branch; incorporating that dependence restores exact centering and false-positive control. Third, independent truth-labelled calibration and model-misspecification experiments separate formula effects from absolute test validity. Two empirical applications show the diagnostic at different biological scales: it localizes the contrast between ribosomal-protein and 16S rRNA signal in deep Tree-of-Life alignments, and identifies short-window losses concentrated at third codon positions in an accession-versioned hepatitis C virus reference panel.

The work should interest readers developing or applying phylogenomic, comparative-genomic and sequence-analysis methods because it turns an often qualitative concern—loss of historical signal—into a branch- and region-specific diagnostic integrated with IQ-TREE. The manuscript is self-contained, accompanied by source data and code, and supported by a separate Supplementary Information file containing the complete protein, nucleotide, viral-genome and methodological controls.
